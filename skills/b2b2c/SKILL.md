# B2B2C and B2C2B

Two motions that both route through someone else's relationship.

---

## B2C2B — the individual carries it into work

Notion's term. **Over half of Notion's B2B customers started as personal users**,
and in May 2020 they made the personal plan completely free specifically to feed
this. See `cases/plg-saas.md`.

The mechanism: someone falls in love with a tool for personal use, then brings it
to their team, then the team brings it to the company. The company never ran a
sales process; an employee did.

**Slack ran the same motion differently**: the freemium tier let teams adopt it
**without IT or procurement**, using a work email address. Bypassing enterprise
purchasing is the growth mechanism, not a side effect.

What it requires:
- The product is genuinely useful for **one person alone**. If it needs a team to
  be valuable, this motion does not exist.
- **Zero friction to start.** No sales contact, no demo gate, no credit card.
- A natural moment where inviting a colleague is the obvious next step.
- Pricing that lets a team form before anyone has to ask for budget.

## Subsidize tomorrow's buyers

Notion went **free for students** on the explicit thesis that they graduate and
"pound the table and demand to use the tools they love," and that IT departments
bend. They also gave accelerator credits through YC, 500 Startups, and Techstars,
resulting in **28% of funded Crunchbase startups** using Notion.

**Loyalty compounds.** Subsidizing people who cannot pay yet is a distribution
investment with a multi-year payback, and it is unusually available to a company
with more time than money.

## B2B2C — reaching consumers through a business

You sell to a business; their customers are your users.

**What makes it attractive:** you acquire users in blocks rather than individually,
and the partner's brand does the trust work.

**What makes it dangerous:**
- You do not own the customer relationship, and often not the data
- The partner can replace you, and will if you become strategic to them
- Your growth rate is capped by their sales cycle, which you do not control
- Support expectations are set by them and paid for by you

**Rules:**
- **Never let one partner exceed ~25% of revenue.** That is not a partnership, it
  is a dependency with an expiry date.
- Negotiate for **direct user relationships** — your branding in the experience,
  your support channel, or your own account layer.
- Build the direct motion in parallel from day one, even if it is small.

## The platform variant

Becoming a component in someone else's product is the strongest version, because
replacement is expensive.

- **shadcn/ui** became the default UI library inside AI codegen tools
- **Supabase and Vercel** became a default pair, each carrying the other
- Stripe is inside checkout flows nobody thinks about

**The move: be the easiest thing to include.** Permissive license, zero-config
default, small surface area, documentation someone can copy without reading. See
`skills/partnerships`.

## Common failure

Treating a B2B2C partner as a channel you can run. You cannot. Their roadmap,
their sales cycle, and their priorities govern your growth, and none of them
answer to you. Plan and staff accordingly, and keep the direct motion alive.
