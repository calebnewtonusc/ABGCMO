# Creator partnerships

## The rule that decides everything

**Sponsor power users, not readers of scripts.**

Notion's creator program sponsored people who already used the product obsessively
rather than paying creators to read copy. The difference is audible to viewers,
and viewers punish the difference. Gymshark selected ambassadors who had built
organic followings through their own fitness journeys, gave them gear and access,
and let them make whatever they wanted.

Glossier: **8% of sales linked directly to ambassador efforts**, with UGC making up
33% of in-feed Instagram posts and 42% on TikTok [A].

## How Notion found them

Notion noticed **700,000 people from one country** visiting the site and traced it
to a single YouTube video [A]. That was the trigger for building a creator program
at all — they followed evidence rather than a plan.

**Check your referrer data before designing a program.** Someone may already be
sending you traffic.

## Scaling the sourcing

Notion's team realized big creators have representatives, so **for each creator's
agent they asked for introductions to the rest of that agent's roster**,
exponentially expanding the pipeline from each successful relationship [A].

## Short-form is underpriced

Notion found short-form video had unusually good ROI because **a creator without a
large following can still get over a million views on a single piece** [A]. On
TikTok and Reels, follower count is not a direct ranking factor — every video is
tested on its own merit. So creator selection can optimize for fit and cost rather
than audience size.

## The ubiquity strategy

Notion's stated goal with short-form was **"surround sound"**: a prospect sees a
billboard, sees it on Twitter, hears it from a friend, so that in an enterprise
conversation the reaction is *"I've heard about that from so many people at this
point."*

Not every impression converts. Some build awareness that makes a later conversion
cheap. **Judge campaigns against the goal you set for them**, not against a
universal conversion bar.

## Ambassador programs: pay in exclusivity

Notion's ambassadors received early feature access, founder AMAs, and small event
budgets — perks costing roughly nothing that filtered hard for true believers. The
most valued benefit was **direct access to the team**. Notion hired no product
managers until 2020 because the ambassador community did that job.

Ben Lang's method for starting: bring twenty of the most vocal fans into a Slack
and **spend an hour with each**, asking why they are there and what they want the
community to become. Twenty conversations, not a survey.

See `skills/community-led-growth` for the full pattern.

## Structuring a paid deal

- **Flat fee plus a tracked link** beats pure affiliate for anyone under 100k
  followers; affiliate-only shifts all risk to them and they know it.
- **Buy usage rights.** The right to reuse their content as an ad is often worth
  more than the original post.
- **Never dictate the script.** Give the message, the constraints, and the facts.
- Start with three creators and one measurable goal before scaling.

## Common failure

Paying for follower count. A 5,000-follower creator whose audience is exactly your
buyer outperforms a 500,000-follower creator whose audience is everyone, and costs
a fraction. Select for **audience overlap and genuine use**, in that order.
