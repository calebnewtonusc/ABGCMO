# Paid acquisition

## The precondition

**Paid amplifies what already works. It does not create it.**

Running ads before organic conversion works buys expensive proof that your message
is wrong. Before spending anything:

- [ ] Some organic traction exists — word of mouth, early social proof, or press
- [ ] A landing page converting at or above benchmark (median 4.3% all industries,
      ~7.2% SaaS free trial) [R]
- [ ] You know your activation rate (~37.5% is the benchmark) [R]
- [ ] You have retention data, or you know you do not

Cursor reached **$100M ARR in 20 months with reportedly no marketing spend** [R].
That is not an argument against ads; it is an argument that the product and the
word of mouth come first.

## Use payback period, not LTV/CAC

For early-stage companies **LTV is a guess**, because you do not have enough
retention history to compute it. Multiplying a guess by a real number produces a
confident-looking fiction.

| Metric | Target | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| CAC payback period | under 12 months | [R] |
| CAC payback, DTC/ecommerce | under 6 months ideal | [R] |
| Early-stage observed payback | ~4.8 months (early adopters convert easily) | [R] |

That early-stage number is a trap: your first cohort is the easiest you will ever
buy. Payback will get worse as you scale past the people who were already looking.

## Getting signal cheaply

- **300-500 EUR across 2-3 creatives** is enough for directional signal [A].
- Budget roughly 10-20% of available growth funds for experiments before committing.
- **Change one variable at a time.** Most early ad testing is uninterpretable
  because audience, creative, and landing page all moved together.
- Test the **message** first, not the channel. If no message works on one channel,
  more channels will not help.

## Where to start, by motion

| Motion | First channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High-intent, known category | Search | People are already looking; you capture, not create, demand |
| New category | Social / content | Nobody is searching for a thing they cannot name |
| Enterprise B2B | LinkedIn, narrow | Expensive per click, viable only with high ACV |
| Developer tools | **Usually none** | Developers block ads and distrust them. Docs SEO and registries beat paid. |

That last row is important and under-said: for most developer tools, paid
acquisition is close to a waste, and the same money spent on documentation
compounds instead.

## Creative

The ad is the message. Most failures blamed on targeting are creative failures.

- Lead with the specific problem in the customer's words
- One claim per ad
- Landing page must match the ad's headline, image, and tone. Mismatch is the
  single most common conversion killer.
- Reading level matters: pages at 5th-7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%
  versus 5.3% for college-level copy [A]

## When to stop

Set the stopping rule before you spend: **what CAC by what date means this channel
is wrong?** Without it, paid budgets run forever on the argument that they need
more optimization.

## Common failure

Scaling a channel that works at $1k/month to $20k/month and expecting linear
results. Audiences saturate, and the cheapest customers are always bought first.
Expect payback to degrade and re-check the stopping rule at every 3-5x in spend.
