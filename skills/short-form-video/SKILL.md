# Short-form and video

## The structural gift: follower count is not a ranking factor

On TikTok and Instagram Reels, distribution runs on an **interest graph, not a
social graph**. Every video is shown to a small test pool first and expands based
on how that pool behaves. A creator with zero followers can out-reach a
100k-follower account, and a first video can go wide.

This means there is **no audience-size moat to climb first**. The content is the
entire variable. That is unusually good news for someone starting.

## TikTok

| Signal | Value |
|---|---|
| Watch time + completion rate share of ranking | 40-50% |
| Target completion rate | ~70% |
| Strongest signals | shares, saves, comments |
| Weakest signal | likes |

**Completion percentage matters more than total watch time.** A 15-second video
watched to the end outranks a 60-second video with more absolute seconds but
lower completion. The single most reliable improvement available to most creators
is *making the video shorter*.

First three seconds decide it. Cut anything that does not hold attention. Use
original audio. Write searchable captions.

## Instagram / Reels

- Separate ranking model per surface (Feed, Reels, Stories, Explore), unified by a
  per-account trust score.
- **DM shares carry the most weight for unconnected reach** — stated directly by
  Adam Mosseri. Design for "someone sends this to a friend," not "someone likes it."
- Completion rate is the strongest Reels signal.
- Accounts under 50k are described as the sweet spot for organic growth.
- Under 10k: treat Reels as the entire strategy for a quarter, then add carousels
  for retention once an audience exists.

## YouTube

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| CTR baseline | 4-8%; 10%+ strong; **under 2% means packaging is wrong** |
| CTR, browse, new channel | 5-8% |
| CTR, browse, established | 7-12% |
| Average view duration | >50% strong, >70% exceptional |

Satisfaction and retention quality now outrank raw watch-time accumulation. Every
upload is tested on its own merit, so small channels do get discovered.

Shorts put content in front of people who would never find long-form, which makes
them the cheapest top-of-funnel a channel has.

## The MrBeast method, generalized

The most transferable idea in creator growth, and it applies far beyond video:

**Package before you produce.** MrBeast makes up to **20 thumbnail variations**
per video and shoots thumbnails *during* filming to capture real emotion. CTR is
treated as the dictator: perfect retention fails if nobody clicks.

**Retention is engineered, not hoped for.** Re-hook every **20-40 seconds** with
rising stakes.

**The part everyone skips:** he spent roughly **five years on low-view uploads**
(2012-2016) studying the platform before finding the format that worked. The
compounding came after. Anyone promising faster than that is selling something.

Generalize it: decide the title and the hook — the promise — before you build the
thing. If you cannot write a compelling headline for it, the problem is upstream
of execution.

## The personality lever

Duolingo went from 50k to 10M+ TikTok followers with a mascot, not a brand
account. Duo's chaotic, funny, un-corporate presence made a language app
culturally relevant. Platform-native content, never repurposed ads.

A mascot or persona can say things a company cannot say in its own name. If your
brand voice is constrained, this is the workaround.
