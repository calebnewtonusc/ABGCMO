# Partnerships and integrations

Distribution you rent rather than build. Among the highest-return, lowest-creativity
moves available, and consistently underused by technical founders who would rather
build than negotiate.

## Integration marketplaces

The Shopify App Store has been named a **#1 growth channel** by some SaaS
companies [A]. Salesforce AppExchange plays the same role in enterprise. For a
SaaS in the $3-6M range, platform integrations are treated as a growth stage
rather than a nice-to-have [A].

**Pick the platform by where your customers already are**, not by size. A listing
in a store your buyer never opens is a build with no distribution attached.

Order of operations:
1. Confirm your customers actually use the platform. Ask ten of them.
2. Build the integration that removes a real step, not the one that is easiest.
3. Optimize the listing like a landing page — the first screenshot and the first
   line do the work.
4. Get reviews from customers you already have. Listing rank is review-sensitive.

## The Zapier pattern

Zapier generates ~**2.6M organic visits/month** from programmatic "connect X to Y"
pages [R]. Every integration you ship is a page that ranks for a query someone is
already typing. The integration is the product; the page is the channel.

If you have integrations and no pages for them, you have built the asset and
skipped the distribution.

## Co-marketing

The star-history playbook: **genuinely promote other people, companies, and
projects, and they promote you back.** Write the listicle that includes them, then
tag them when you share it. Many will amplify, and the reach compounds.

This works because it costs them nothing and flatters them accurately. It stops
working the moment it is transparently transactional.

## Ecosystem partnerships

The strongest version: become a component in someone else's stack.

- **shadcn/ui** became the default UI library inside AI codegen tools. That is
  distribution no campaign could buy, and it happened because the copy-paste model
  made it trivially adoptable.
- **Supabase and Vercel** became a default pair, each carrying the other.
- **obra/superpowers** inflected on an official registry listing.

**The move: make yourself the easiest thing to include.** Permissive license,
zero-config default, small surface area, and documentation someone can copy
without reading.

## What kills partnerships

- **Asking before you have anything.** Partnerships are for amplifying traction,
  not creating it. A partner is deciding whether to spend their credibility.
- **Vague asks.** "Let's explore synergies" gets nothing. "Can we be listed in
  your integrations directory, here is the PR" gets a yes or a no.
- **No owner.** Partnerships die from neglect more than rejection.
- **Reciprocity you cannot deliver.** Promising to promote something to an
  audience you do not have.

## Common failure

Building a partnership program before having 100 happy customers. The partner's
first question is who already uses this, and there is no good answer yet.
