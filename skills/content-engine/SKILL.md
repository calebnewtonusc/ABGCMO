# Content engine

## Post findings, not announcements

"I built X" is an ad and reads like one. It asks the reader for attention and
offers nothing first.

**The strongest position available to an unknown account is a specific, hard-won,
undocumented finding.** Something that cost you real time to learn and is not in
the documentation. Those get bookmarked, and bookmarks are heavily weighted on
every platform that has them.

The product link goes in the reply. The finding earns the click; the announcement
begs for it.

## The four hooks that work for technical audiences

1. **Contrarian claim** against received wisdom. "Show HN has no measurable effect"
   beats "how to launch on HN."
2. **A surprising number.** Specific, sourced, checkable.
3. **A shared frustration named precisely.** The reader thinks "I thought it was
   just me."
4. **Reference material.** Lists and data threads get shared because people want
   to find them again.

The hook is most of it. A thread with mediocre content and a magnetic hook
outperforms the reverse, every time.

## Package before you produce

MrBeast makes up to **20 thumbnail variations** per video and shoots thumbnails
during filming rather than after. CTR is treated as the dictator: perfect
retention fails if nobody clicks.

Generalized: **decide the title and the promise before you build the thing.** If
you cannot write a compelling headline for it, the problem is upstream of
execution and no amount of polish fixes it.

## Structure

Threads and long posts follow the magazine shape:
1. **Hook** — stops the scroll
2. **Context** — sets up the stakes in one or two lines
3. **Body** — delivers, one idea per unit
4. **Close** — lands on the concrete point or the next action

Length: threads of 5-9 posts perform around 3x a single post on X. One to three a
week; more dilutes quality.

## Repurpose by reshaping, never by copying

The three-phase loop: **pillar** (one rich source), **atomize** (break into
components), **distribute** (ship each one natively).

But native means rewritten, not reformatted:

| Platform | What the same idea becomes |
|---|---|
| Newsletter / blog | The full argument with sources — the canonical asset |
| X | The single sharpest finding, as a thread; link in the reply |
| LinkedIn | The narrative version: a decision and what happened |
| LinkedIn carousel | 7-10 slides, portrait 4:5 — highest-engagement format there |
| Reddit | Framed as a lesson, tool as context |
| Short-form video | The one counterintuitive claim, in under 30 seconds |

**Verbatim duplication is associated with ~65% organic traffic decline after six
months.** Use canonical tags pointing at your own domain when syndicating.

## Cadence, honestly

More is not better past a point, and burnout is the most common failure.

- **X:** 2-3 posts a week plus 10-15 replies a day. The replies do more work than
  the posts.
- **LinkedIn:** 1-2 posts a week plus 10-15 substantive comments a day.
- **Newsletter:** weekly or biweekly, held for at least 12 months.
- **YouTube:** weekly, and fewer good videos beat many mediocre ones.

If a cadence cannot survive a bad week, it is the wrong cadence. Pick the one you
will still be running in six months, because none of this works in less than that.

## Writing that does not sound like everyone else

- Cut the throat-clearing. Open on the point.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" constructions. State Y.
- Specific numbers beat adjectives. "Cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 4" beats
  "improved efficiency."
- **Portability test:** if a sentence could move unchanged to another project or
  person, it is filler. Cut it or make it true only here.
- Say what the thing does not do. Naming a limitation buys more credibility than
  any claim.
