# Community-led growth

## The precondition nobody states

**A product with no learning curve generates no community.**

Notion is semi-difficult on purpose: easy enough to start, hard enough that
mastery is scarce. **That gap is what creates demand** for guides, templates,
courses, and consultants — which is to say, the gap *is* the community. If your
product is trivially simple, there is nothing for a community to be expert about,
and no amount of Discord management will manufacture one.

Ask this before building community: what is there to get good at?

## Hire the fans who are already doing the work for free

The single best move in the whole discipline, and it is nearly free.

Ben Lang built notionpages.com as a fan — a gallery of Notion workspace
screenshots pulling 20-40k monthly visitors. Notion **hired him** and put him in
charge of the official gallery. He then built the ambassador program.

Haebom Lee built a 43,000-member Notion community in Korea. Fans wrote textbooks
and ran forums; a few became certified company consultants.

**Look for the people already evangelizing without being asked.** They make better
advocates than anyone you can pay, because paid creators do it for the money and
fans do it because it is true.

## Pay in exclusivity, not cash

Notion's ambassadors received early feature access, founder AMAs, and small budgets
to host events. **Perks costing roughly nothing that filter hard for true
believers.** The most valued benefit was direct access to the team.

Notion hired **no product managers until 2020**. The ambassador community did that
job, surfacing use cases and testing features before public release. Engineers
watched the discussions directly.

Ben Lang's method when starting: bring twenty of the most vocal fans into a Slack
and **spend an hour with each one**, asking "why are you here?" and "what do you
want this community to look like?" Not a survey. Twenty conversations.

## Select ambassadors for credibility, not follower count

Gymshark chose people who had built organic followings through their own fitness
journeys, supplied them gear, invited them to events, and let them make whatever
they wanted. Glossier used micro-influencers and everyday users; **8% of sales
linked directly to ambassador efforts**, and UGC made up 33% of in-feed Instagram
posts and 42% on TikTok.

Notion sponsored creators who were **power users**, rather than paying creators to
read scripts. The difference is audible to viewers.

## The ceiling nobody warns you about

**Glossier's organic growth plateaued** once it had converted most of its natural
audience from the Into The Gloss blog and Instagram following. Community-led growth
has a ceiling set by the size of the community, and the transition to paid
acquisition afterward is genuinely hard because the paid channel does not carry
the same trust.

Plan for the transition before you hit the ceiling.

## Addition vs. multiplication

Borrowed from church growth models, and it generalizes cleanly.

**Addition:** one large centralized organization, a charismatic center,
administrative systems. Grows in proportion to your capacity to run things.

**Multiplication:** a group grows to a healthy size and then **divides**. Ordinary
members do the core activity rather than routing everything through the center.
Grows in proportion to your capacity to hand things off.

**If you are the bottleneck in every unit, you have chosen addition whether you
meant to or not.** One 5,000-person Discord where you answer everything is not the
same asset as fifty 100-person communities that run themselves.

## Discord specifics

Discord surpassed Slack and forums for developer communities on flexibility,
role-based access, and persistent history. But: **communities do not grow because
you have a server.** They grow because members have a clear reason to join,
contribute, and come back tomorrow.

Measurement: track engagement, content creation, and sentiment for the first 6
months. Attributed signups, retention lift, and support deflection only become
meaningful at 6-12 months. Judging a young community on pipeline kills it early.
