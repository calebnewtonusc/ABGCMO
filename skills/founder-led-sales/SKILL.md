# Founder-led sales

## Warm beats cold by an order of magnitude

| Path | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Warm introduction | 30-50% |
| Cold outreach | 2-5% |

Your existing network almost certainly holds 5-10 potential customers or people
who can introduce you to them. **Exhaust that completely before buying strangers.**
Mercor's first clients were the founder's Georgetown classmates; nearly every
early marketplace customer came through one student founder community.

One good conversation at an event is worth roughly 50 cold emails.

## Cold email that works

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Average reply rate | 3.43% |
| Above 5% | ahead of most B2B senders |
| **8-12%** | **elite, roughly top decile** |
| Referencing a specific buying signal | 15-25% |

**Deliverability sets the ceiling, not copy.** A large gap between sends and
replies is an infrastructure problem, and rewriting the email will not fix it.

Setup, non-negotiable:
- Secondary sending domains, authenticated with **SPF, DKIM, and DMARC**
- **~3 weeks of warmup** before scaling volume
- **50-100 emails per mailbox per day**, maximum
- Bounce rate under 3% (best in class under 1.5%); spam complaints under 0.1%

Copy:
- **50-125 words.** Roughly 50% higher reply rates than longer formats.
- Reference something specific and recent: a funding round, a leadership change,
  a hiring surge, a shipped feature. Generic positioning statements get ignored.
- **One benefit per message.** Multiple claims create skepticism, not enthusiasm.
- Monday is best for launching sequences; Wednesday peaks for follow-ups.

**Follow up.** 58% of replies come from step one, which means **42% come from
follow-ups**. 80% of sales require 5+ touches and most founders stop after two.
This is the cheapest improvement available to almost everyone.

## The Collison installation

Stripe's founders did not ask "will you try our beta?" When someone agreed, they
said **"right then, give me your laptop"** and set it up on the spot.

Generalize it: **remove every step between interest and value.** If someone says
yes, do the setup for them, right now, while they are interested. Interest decays
faster than any other resource you have.

## Remove the blockers, not just the objections

Mercor handled contracts, tax forms, and international currency transfers for
their clients — the actual reason companies avoided overseas contractors. That was
not a feature, it was the removal of the thing blocking the sale. Staying in the
loop also gave them the retention data that trained their matching model.

Ask: what is the boring operational reason this person will not buy? Do that work
yourself.

## Timeline and volume

- 4-8 weeks from first outreach to first paying customer.
- Refine messaging in the first 2 weeks; expect a repeatable pitch by week 4.
- 50-100 sends per day is the solo-founder sweet spot. More requires templates
  that degrade reply rates; fewer produces pipeline too slowly to learn from.

## When to hire sales

Once you have **10-20 accounts generating $50k+ annually** through self-serve or
CS-led expansion.

The sequence: founder runs the first 5-10 enterprise deals personally and
**documents the playbook**. Hire one experienced AE who has closed $50k+ deals in
a similar category. They co-run deals with the founder for 90 days, then take the
playbook solo.

Too early kills PLG momentum. Too late leaves obvious money on the table. Slack,
Notion, Figma, and Linear all converged on PLG as top-of-funnel with sales as the
closing motion above a usage threshold.
