# Brand

## What brand does for a company with no budget

Brand is not a logo and it is not awareness advertising, which you cannot afford.
For a small company brand is **the reason someone remembers you and repeats your
name accurately to a colleague.** That is a distribution function.

The measurable version: can a user describe what you do, in their own words,
correctly, to someone else? Notion's word of mouth ran on one line — *"I cancelled
Evernote, Trello, and our wiki."* If your users cannot repeat your pitch, you have
no word of mouth regardless of how good the product is.

## Design as the differentiator

Linear, Figma, Superhuman, Notion, Arc, and Attio are the same argument repeated:
in a category where features converge, **craft is a durable differentiator**
because it is expensive to copy and immediately visible.

This is unusually available to small teams. A two-person team can out-design a
200-person company, and cannot out-feature one.

## Personality can go where a company voice cannot

Duolingo went from 50k to over 10M TikTok followers with a mascot, not a brand
account. Duo's chaotic, funny, un-corporate presence made a language app culturally
relevant to Gen Z [A].

A mascot or persona can say things a company cannot say in its own name. If your
brand voice is constrained by enterprise buyers or by your own discomfort, this is
the workaround.

## Naming

- **Searchable.** If your name is a common word, you will never rank for it.
- **Spellable from hearing it once.** Word of mouth is verbal.
- **Check trademarks before you have anything to lose.** Lucasfilm has a documented
  history of cease-and-desists over Star Wars marks, including trademarking "droid"
  and forcing renames. A project you intend to grow is exactly the kind that gets
  noticed. See `skills/anti-patterns`.
- **Check the .com, the GitHub org, and the handle** in the same sitting.
- Descriptive names help early comprehension; abstract names age better. Under
  ambiguity, favor comprehension — you need to be understood before you need to
  be timeless.

## Consistency beats cleverness

Pick a small set of decisions and hold them: one typeface pairing, one accent
color, one voice. Recognition comes from repetition, and repetition is free.

The most common small-company brand failure is not ugliness, it is **variance** —
every surface looking like a different company.

## Voice

Write like the smartest person on your team explaining something to a colleague:

- Specific numbers over adjectives. "Cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 4" beats
  "improved efficiency."
- Say what the product does **not** do. Naming a limitation buys credibility on
  everything else, and it is the fastest trust-building move available.
- No throat-clearing, no closing aphorisms, no "excited to announce."
- **Portability test:** if a sentence could move unchanged to a competitor's site,
  it is filler.

## Common failure

Brand work as a substitute for positioning. A beautiful site describing a product
nobody understands converts worse than an ugly site with a clear category line.
Run `skills/positioning` first; brand amplifies a clear message and cannot rescue
a muddy one.
