# PR and earned media

## The reframe

You cannot pitch "we exist." Journalists and newsletter writers are not looking
for your announcement; they are looking for **a story they can publish that their
readers will finish**. Your job is to hand them one that happens to contain you.

## What is actually a story

| Not a story | Is a story |
|---|---|
| We launched | A number nobody has published before |
| We raised money | A trend with your data as the evidence |
| We shipped a feature | Something surprising you learned running this |
| We are excited | A conflict, a reversal, or a thing everyone believes that is wrong |

The best earned media most technical founders can get is **original data**. You
have logs nobody else has. A post titled "we analyzed N of X and found Y" is
publishable; a launch announcement is not.

## Where to aim, in order of realism

1. **Niche newsletters.** Smallest audience, highest conversion, easiest yes.
   Often one person who reads every email. Start here.
2. **Podcasts.** 54% of podcast listeners find shows through other podcasts [A],
   and guests bring their own networks. Pitch shows with genuine audience overlap.
3. **Trade and vertical press.** Cares about your industry specifically.
4. **Aggregators** — Hacker News, relevant subreddits. See `skills/reddit-hn`.
5. **Tier-one tech press.** Realistically requires funding news, a notable name,
   or a story so good they come to you. Do not build a plan around it.

## The pitch

Short, specific, and pre-written for them.

```
Subject: [specific number or finding], not a pitch

[Name] — you wrote about [specific piece, recently].

We [did the thing] and found [specific number]. [One line on why it is
surprising or counterintuitive.]

Happy to send the full dataset, or just answer questions. No need to mention us
if the finding is the interesting part.

[You]
```

The last line does more work than the rest. It signals you want the story told,
not the plug placed, and it is the difference between a pitch and a source.

## Rules

- **Read three of their recent pieces first.** Reference one specifically. Mass
  pitches are visible and get you filtered permanently.
- **One outlet at a time for a real exclusive**, or be upfront that it is not one.
- **Have the assets ready**: screenshots, a founder photo, the raw data, a
  one-paragraph description they can paste.
- **Respond within the hour.** Journalists work on deadlines and the second-fastest
  source is not a source.
- **Never ask to review the piece before publication.** It marks you as
  inexperienced and ends the relationship.

## Timing

Tie a pitch to something already in the news cycle. A finding about developer
security is a story the week after a major breach and a filed email the week
before.

## Common failure

Treating PR as a launch activity. Relationships with three newsletter writers
built over six months produce more than a blast to two hundred journalists on
launch day, and the relationships keep paying.
