# Customer success and expansion

## Why this is a growth skill

**Net revenue retention above 100% means the company grows without acquiring
anyone.** Top PLG performers run 110-130% NRR; SMB-focused median is 100-105% [R].
At 130% NRR, existing customers alone produce 30% annual growth.

That is usually cheaper than any acquisition channel, and it is the thing most
teams under-resource because it feels like maintenance rather than growth.

## Support is a growth channel

Notion's founders answered support messages in **about a minute**, which converted
complainers into evangelists on the spot. Ivan Zhao personally answered tweets and
emails, which also gave him the clearest possible read on the product's problems.
**99% of Notion's growth was word of mouth.** See `cases/plg-saas.md`.

Response time is the variable most under a small team's control and the one that
most reliably produces advocates.

## Expansion, in order of ease

1. **More seats.** Easiest. Requires the product to be more valuable with more
   people in it. Notion, Slack, Figma all expand this way by construction.
2. **More usage.** Usage-based pricing expands automatically as customers succeed,
   which aligns your revenue with their outcome.
3. **More product.** Cross-sell an adjacent module. Hardest; it is a new sale to a
   warm buyer.

Design pricing so success produces expansion without a conversation. See
`skills/pricing-packaging`.

## The health signals that predict churn

Watch leading indicators, not the renewal date:

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Usage trend, not usage level** | A high-usage account declining is more at risk than a stable low-usage one |
| **Breadth of adoption** | One user is a churn risk; five across two teams is not |
| **Champion departure** | The single strongest churn predictor in B2B |
| **Support ticket sentiment** | Frustration precedes cancellation by weeks |
| **Time since last login by admin** | Cheap and surprisingly predictive |

## Cohorts, not blended numbers

Blended churn mixes your best customers from three years ago with last month's
wrong-fit signups. It describes the average and represents nobody.

**Later cohorts should outperform earlier ones at the same age.** If they do not,
you are acquiring worse-fit customers, and that is an acquisition problem wearing
a retention costume. See `skills/metrics` for the cohort table.

## Turning customers into distribution

- **Ask for the referral at the moment of success**, not at renewal. Right after
  they hit an outcome is the only moment the ask is easy.
- **Case studies are sales assets and SEO assets.** Interview the customer about
  the problem, not the product.
- **The people who already evangelize unpaid are your best hires.** Notion hired
  Ben Lang from the community. See `skills/community-led-growth`.

## When to hire for this

The founder does it until roughly 10-20 accounts. Then one person who owns
retention and expansion, before an AE who owns net-new. Expansion revenue is
cheaper than acquisition, so staffing acquisition first is usually backwards.

## Common failure

Measuring CS on ticket volume or response SLA. Those measure activity. Measure
**net revenue retention and logo retention by cohort**, which measure whether the
function works.
