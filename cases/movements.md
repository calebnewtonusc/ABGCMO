# Movements, nonprofits, and organizing

Growth outside a commercial context. The mechanics are the same; the currency is
participation rather than revenue.

## The ALS Ice Bucket Challenge

Raised **over $115 million**. The mechanism is worth dissecting because it is the
purest nomination loop ever run:

- The action was **physically performable and filmable** — content generated itself.
- **Participants tagged specific people by name**, which converts a broadcast into
  a personal obligation. This is the entire engine.
- Refusing was socially visible, so the loop had teeth.
- The hashtag made the whole thing legible as one event rather than scattered posts.

**Transferable:** a nomination is not a share. A share is a broadcast anyone can
ignore. A nomination is addressed to a named person in public, and the conversion
rate is not comparable.

## charity: water

Storytelling and **radical transparency** as the growth engine: interactive impact
maps and video showing where specific donations went, in near real time. The 100%
model (operating costs covered separately so every donated dollar goes to
projects) removed the single biggest objection to giving.

**Transferable:** in any category where trust is the bottleneck, removing the
objection structurally beats arguing against it in copy.

## The research-first nonprofit turnaround

A worked example worth copying wholesale. Before writing a single word of new
copy, the team spent **six weeks** on: three years of donor database analytics,
volunteer retention mapped at 30/60/90 days, website behavior by traffic source,
and **22 one-on-one interviews** with current participants, lapsed donors, active
volunteers, and foundation contacts.

Two findings inverted their strategy:
- **Donors were not motivated by program statistics.** They wanted evidence that
  lives changed.
- **Volunteers were not leaving because they were busy.** They were leaving
  because their skills were underused.

Both are things no dashboard would have surfaced and no amount of A/B testing
would have found. **Transferable: the constraint is often not what the metrics
say it is, and 22 conversations costs less than a quarter of wrong work.**

## Church multiplication — addition vs. multiplication

Two models, and the distinction generalizes far beyond churches.

**The megachurch model:** a charismatic founder, one large centralized
organization, administrative systems resembling a business. Grows by **addition**.
Roughly two dozen US megachurches in 1990; over 1,300 today.

**The multiplication model:** treats a centralized organization as structurally
unable to use the full capacity of its people. A group grows to a healthy,
sustainable size and then **divides**. Numbers grow far faster through
multiplication than addition, and the model works by unleashing ordinary people
to do the core activity rather than routing everything through the center.

The multisite variant — "one church in multiple locations under one leadership
and budget" — is the hybrid.

**Transferable:** this is the same choice as centralized product versus community
chapters, or one big Discord versus many small ones. Addition scales with your
capacity to run things. Multiplication scales with your capacity to hand things
off. If the founder is the bottleneck in every unit, you have chosen addition
whether you meant to or not.

## Campus and community organizing

The Tinder sorority tour (`marketplaces.md`) and Notion's student program are the
same move: **find a pre-existing dense network with a natural convener, and get
the convener to demonstrate the thing to the room.** You are not acquiring
individuals; you are acquiring an already-assembled network in one action.

The prerequisites: the network must already meet, someone in it must have
standing, and the payoff must be visible in the room within minutes.
