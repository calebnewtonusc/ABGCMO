# Developer tools

---

## Stripe

**Mechanism:** documentation as go-to-market, plus the Collison installation.

The "**seven lines of code**" positioning was a *product* decision, not a
marketing one. It shaped the API design, the docs, and the support model.
Primary growth channel for the first five years was **developer word of mouth**:
developers who had suffered legacy payment integrations told their peers, and the
contrast did the selling.

**The Collison installation.** Named at YC after the founders' technique. Diffident
founders ask "will you try our beta?" The Collisons said **"give me your laptop"**
and set it up on the spot. This is the canonical unscalable move.

---

## Twilio

Same shape: comprehensive, navigable, developer-first documentation as the primary
acquisition asset. For most devtool companies documentation pages drive **40-60%
of total organic traffic**, and most teams treat docs as a chore rather than the
largest channel they own.

## Cursor (Anysphere)

**$100M ARR within 20 months of launch, reportedly with no marketing spend.**
Then $500M by June 2025, $1B by November 2025, $2B by February 2026, ~$4B by
June 2026. Over 1M daily active users, all organic.

Mechanisms:
- **Free tier as top of funnel:** 2,000 completions/month.
- **Deliberately hard to reach for enterprises.** They removed contact options
  from the site to stay focused on individual developers while enterprise
  interest was rising. That is a costly, deliberate focus decision.
- Custom models for autocomplete and cross-file prediction — speed was the product.
- ~100 employees, ~90% engineers and researchers.

**Transferable:** saying no to the enterprise money for a while is what kept the
individual-developer experience good enough to spread on its own.

## Where a devtool actually gets found

Ranked by durability rather than speed:

1. **Registries and marketplaces.** One listing, permanent, and it is where
   people with intent already look.
2. **Documentation SEO.** 40-60% of organic traffic. Slow, compounding, largest.
3. **Awesome-lists.** Small individually, permanent, and they compound: more
   stars gets you into more lists, which gets more stars.
4. **A concentrated launch day.** HN + Reddit + X + LinkedIn on one day, because
   trending ranks velocity.
5. **Word of mouth from a genuinely better experience.** Not a channel you can
   run, but the only one that scales without you.
