# Consumer and social

---

## Wordle — the purest viral mechanic ever shipped

**90 players in November 2021. Over 2,000,000 by January 2022.** No marketing
budget, no ads, no growth team. Josh Wardle built it for one person.

**The mechanism was not the game. It was the share format.** After finishing, you
could copy a **spoiler-free emoji grid** — your guess pattern, without your
guesses or the answer.

Study why this is so well designed:
- It solved the problem that kills most game virality: *how do I brag without
  ruining it for you?* Sharing became **socially safe**.
- Every shared grid was a **peer recommendation** that contained no ask.
- It was **plain text**, so it traveled anywhere: group chats, Twitter, Slack.
- **One puzzle per day, the same for everyone.** Scarcity capped how much time it
  could take, which paradoxically made people *more* likely to return daily.

**Transferable, and this is the highest-leverage idea in the file:** the shareable
artifact should encode your result without spoiling the experience, require no
account, and be socially safe to post. Most products bolt on a "share" button
that broadcasts an ad. Wordle shipped a share format that broadcast *the person
sharing it*.

## Spotify Wrapped

An annual data feature that became a cultural event and reportedly drives over
**2 billion annual impressions**. It reframes data collection as a *benefit*
rather than defending it, generates FOMO in non-users, and turns existing users
into ambassadors on a fixed calendar date.

It was copied so widely that "Wrapped" is now a genre: Strava, Beli, SoulCycle,
and equivalents across fitness, travel, finance, education, and ecommerce.

MAU rose 11% YoY to 751 million in a period where Wrapped was named the main driver.

**Transferable:** if you hold data about someone's year, you are sitting on an
annual growth event. The rule is that the output must flatter the *user*, not
the product.

## Duolingo

**TikTok followers: 50k to over 10 million.** DAUs from ~4.9M (2019) to over 80M
(late 2024). Revenue ~$200M (2022) to $400M+ (2024). One hashtag campaign
reported a 39% CTR.

Two engines:
1. **A mascot with a personality**, not a brand account. Duo's chaotic, funny,
   deeply un-corporate TikTok presence made a language app culturally relevant to
   Gen Z. Platform-native content, not repurposed ads.
2. **Streaks.** The longer the streak, the higher the psychological cost of
   breaking it. Combined with leagues and aggressive reminders, this creates
   genuine daily-habit retention. They later ran an entire global campaign about
   *reviving lost streaks*, which is a retention mechanic marketed as a story.

**Transferable:** a mascot can carry a brand voice that a company cannot say in
its own name. And a streak is the cheapest retention mechanic that exists — but
it only works if the daily action is genuinely small.

## Clubhouse

Focused on bringing in **the right early users** rather than the most users,
using invite-gating to control composition. The cautionary half: exclusivity
generates a spike, and if the underlying retention is not there, the spike is
all you get. Contrast with Superhuman, where the waitlist fed a product people
kept paying $30/month for.

## Instagram / Reels, TikTok — for anyone starting from zero

The structurally important fact on both: **follower count is not a direct ranking
factor.** These are interest graphs, not social graphs. Every video is tested on a
small pool first and expands on performance. A first video can out-reach a
100k-follower account.

What that means practically: on these platforms, the *content* is the entire
variable. There is no audience-size moat to climb first. Completion rate is the
strongest signal on both; DM shares carry the most weight for unconnected reach
on Instagram; likes are the weakest signal on both.
