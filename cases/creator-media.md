## MrBeast

The most instructive thing about MrBeast is the part nobody quotes: he spent
roughly **five years (2012-2016) on low-view uploads**, studying the platform
obsessively, before finding his format in 2017. The compounding came after.

**Packaging first.** He creates up to **20 thumbnail variations** per video, with
a team of artists and strategists, and shoots thumbnails *during* the video to
capture real emotion rather than staging it after. CTR is treated as the dictator:
a video with perfect retention fails if the thumbnail does not get the click.

**Retention engineering.** Re-hook the viewer every **20-40 seconds** with rising
stakes. Retention is the strategy; the content is downstream of it.

**Total reinvestment** of revenue into production, plus **dubbing into 40+
languages**, so one video concept earns from North America, South America,
Europe, and Asia simultaneously.

**Transferable to anything, not just video:** package before you produce. Decide
the title and the thumbnail — the *hook and the promise* — before you build the
thing. If you cannot write a compelling headline for it, the problem is upstream
of execution.

## Morning Brew

**100,000 to 1.7 million subscribers in 18 months**, 4.5M today. Roughly **30% of
growth from the referral program** — as much as **80% in the early days**.

Mechanics of "Share the Brew":
- Tiered, branded physical rewards (stickers, then bigger items).
- A personal referral link at the bottom of **every** issue.
- Visible progress toward the next tier, so the loop stays salient.
- **Cost per acquisition around $0.25** — roughly $1.25 in stickers and postage
  for five new subscribers.
- Social and SMS referral conversion **dwarfed** email referrals, so they pushed
  those channels specifically.

**Transferable:** a newsletter referral program is the cheapest growth loop in
media. The physical reward matters more than its value — people want the sticker
because it proves membership.

## Roblox and Fortnite — UGC as the content engine

Roblox: **110M+ daily active users**, **$1.5B+ paid to creators in 2025** (+70%
YoY). Across Roblox, Fortnite, and Overwolf, developer payouts were ~**$2.2B in
2025** (+47% YoY).

The loop: give users powerful creation tools, and they generate a never-ending
free stream of content that keeps other users engaged for years. This collapses
content cost and raises retention simultaneously. UGC titles iterate in real time
with community feedback — daily hotfixes and requested features shipped straight
onto live servers.

Note the economics differ sharply: Fortnite's ~$5,000 average annual payout per
creator is roughly **35x** Roblox's, reflecting a smaller creator base and far
more concentrated earnings.

**Transferable:** if your users can make things for each other, your content
problem and your retention problem are the same problem, and solving it once
solves both. If they cannot, do not pretend a template gallery is a UGC economy.

## Podcasts

- **54% of listeners find new shows through other podcasts.** Cross-promotion is
  the most efficient channel available.
- YouTube drives the most podcast discovery, then Facebook and TikTok — short
  clips reach people who do not know you.
- Practical play: approach 3-5 shows with **similar audience size** and run a
  coordinated promo-swap week.
- Growth is nonlinear: guest spots and swaps create spikes; consistent publishing
  and retention are what make the spikes stick.
